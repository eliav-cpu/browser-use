# Competitive Analysis

Use this workflow to produce high-quality, date-stamped competitive intelligence.

## 1) Set scope before researching

Define:
- Company/product being analyzed
- Named competitors (direct + adjacent)
- Market segment and geography
- Output format (brief, deep-dive, battlecard, matrix)
- Time horizon (e.g., "as of March 27, 2026")

If scope is missing, make the minimum reasonable assumptions and state them explicitly.

## 2) Collect evidence systematically

### Primary sources (competitor-owned)
- Website: homepage, product pages, pricing, about, careers
- Blog/resource center: themes, posting cadence, depth
- Social profiles: messaging, engagement, campaign style
- Product demos/free trial flows: onboarding, UX, key features
- Webinars/events: topics, speakers, audience intent
- Press/newsroom: launches, partnerships, milestones
- Job postings: strategic hiring signals

### Secondary sources (third-party)
- Review sites: G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Product Hunt
- Analyst and market reports (if available)
- News coverage and interviews
- Community sentiment: Reddit, forums, Slack communities
- SEO visibility and keyword gaps
- Public financial filings for public companies

Always capture source URLs and publication dates.

## 3) Organize findings by competitor and dimension

Use this core structure per competitor:
- What they do (1 sentence)
- Target customer
- Pricing model snapshot
- Top strengths
- Key weaknesses
- Notable recent developments

Then organize by comparison dimensions:
- Messaging
- Positioning
- Content strategy
- Product capabilities
- GTM/sales motion

## Value proposition decomposition

For each competitor, document:
- Promise (desired outcome)
- Evidence (proof)
- Mechanism (how it works)
- Claimed uniqueness

## Narrative analysis

Extract each competitor story:
- Villain (status quo/problem)
- Hero (buyer, team, or product)
- Transformation (before/after)
- Stakes (cost of inaction)

## Messaging quality checks

Rate each competitor on:
- Clarity
- Differentiation
- Proof
- Consistency
- Resonance

## 6) Map positioning

Use positioning statement format:

For [target audience], [company/product] is the [category] that [key benefit/differentiator] because [reason to believe].

Build a 2x2 map using the most decision-relevant axes:
- Price vs capability
- Ease of use vs power
- SMB vs enterprise focus
- Point solution vs platform
- Innovative vs established

Identify underserved quadrants and whitespace opportunities.

## 7) Produce battlecards

Create concise one-page battlecards with:
- Competitor + last updated date
- Quick overview (what they do, target customer, pricing)
- Their pitch (tagline + top claims)
- Strengths (be fair and specific)
- Weaknesses (evidence-backed)
- Our differentiators (3-5, each with why-it-matters + proof)
- Objection handling snippets
- Landmines to set (discovery questions)
- Landmines to defuse (prepared responses)
- Win/loss themes

## Objection handling examples

- "[Competitor] does X too" → explain your mechanism and proof differences.
- "[Competitor] is cheaper" → quantify trade-offs and total value.
- "I've heard good things about [Competitor]" → acknowledge strengths, then pivot to fit criteria.

## 8) Synthesize into actionable recommendations

Always end with:
- Top 3 threats
- Top 3 opportunities
- Suggested positioning moves (next 30/90 days)
- Suggested content moves (topics + formats)
- Suggested sales enablement updates

## 9) Date-stamp and maintain cadence

Competitive intel expires quickly. Include:
- "Research completed on: <date>"
- "Data freshness window: <range>"
- "Confidence level: high/medium/low by section"

Recommended cadence:
- Deep analysis: quarterly
- Monitoring refresh: monthly
- Alerts: ongoing for press releases, job postings, and brand mentions
